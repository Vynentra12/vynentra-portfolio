What We Want to Move Away From
The identity should deliberately avoid the visual clichés of renewable energy.
We do not want to rely on:
Generic green colour palettes
Leaves and environmental symbols
Literal sun icons
Lightning bolts
Globe symbols
Generic wind swirls
Literal turbine illustrations as the logo
Overly corporate consulting aesthetics
Excessive technical clutter
These visual codes immediately communicate 
"renewable energy company"
.
Our ambition is stronger:
Build a brand that communicates energy 
intelligence
 without having to illustrate energy.

Company information:
 
Core Business Strategy & Market Gap
Kilowatt-Scale Focus:
 Major industry competitors (Suzlon, Adani, Wind World, Vestas) focus almost exclusively on large megawatt-scale power projects (2–5 MW). The company targets the underserved kilowatt-scale market catering to residential, institutional, and mid-sized commercial clients.
Wind vs. Solar Advantage:
 Wind power provides 24-hour daily energy generation compared to 
solar's
 limited 8–9 hours per day.
Product Portfolio & Specifications
Vertical Axis Wind Turbines (VAWT):
1 kHz Units:
 Generates 8–10 units/day.
5 kHz Units:
 Generates 48–50 kilo-units/day (far exceeding average apartment usage of 300–400 units/month).
Applications:
 Residential complexes, villas, schools, clubhouses, and gyms.
Horizontal Axis Wind Turbines (HAWT):
Capacities:
 Up to 10 kW, reaching heights of 10–15 meters.
Partnership:
 Sourced through an execution partnership with 
a company called 
Unitron.
Captive Power Plants (CPP):
Capacities:
 Large-scale setups starting at 80+ meters in height.
Applications:
 Hotels, industrial areas, and high-consumption commercial clients.
Customer Onboarding & Technical Execution
Site Assessment:
 Clients provide latitude and longitude coordinates so the technical team can determine altitude and calculate local wind speeds using anemometers.
Custom Configurations:
 Systems are fully customizable, including modular turbine arrays (e.g., combining two 2 kW and two 3 kW turbines for a 10 kW requirement) or hybrid wind-solar configurations.
Installation & Maintenance:
 Includes full setup (wiring, distribution boxes) along with ongoing Annual Maintenance Contracts (AMC) to monitor long-term performance.
Manufacturing, Economics & Supply Chain Truths
Local Manufacturing Goals:
 Many competitors buy vertical units from China for ~₹50,000, assemble imported "knockdown" parts, and resell them as "Made in India" for ₹1,00,000–₹1,50,000.
Component Sourcing:
 Permanent magnets must currently be imported from China, but the company aims to establish true domestic manufacturing using carbon 
fiber
 blades to qualify for government subsidies.
CPP Model & Grid Balancing:
 Clients with multi-crore utility bills (e.g., hotel chains consuming ~160 kW) fund remote power plants on outskirts land (e.g., 
Kamshet
). Power is fed into the grid, and offset credits/subsidies reduce their urban electricity bills.
Social Impact Vision:
 Exploring small village setups (~₹3–₹4 Crore initial setup) to supply off-grid rural areas with cheaper power at ₹5–₹6 per unit versus standard ₹10 per unit rates.
Target Markets, Case Studies & Marketing Strategy
Geographic Focus:
 Central Mumbai lacks open space and bungalows for vertical turbines; initial high-converting markets for villa/bungalow models include 
Chandigarh
 and 
Delhi
.
Live Trial & Proof Points:
 The team is currently evaluating a 10 kW test system (composed of two 2 kW and two 3 kW turbines) installed on a school roof over a 6-month period to gather real-world data and capture client video testimonials.
Sales Channels:
Social Media:
 Market small vertical-axis turbines directly to luxury villa/bungalow owners.
Direct B2B Outreach:
 Target high-ticket CPP power plant sales directly through management and corporate meetings.
